# Trace-TriangleAIHackathon

Nonprofits do meaningful work every day, but demonstrating that impact can be surprisingly difficult. Information about programs, activities, volunteers, and outcomes is often scattered across spreadsheets, forms, and documents. When it comes time to apply for grants, organizations must spend valuable time collecting this information and turning it into a compelling, evidence-backed narrative.

We built Trace to address this problem.

Our inspiration came from NOTEWORTHE Music, a nonprofit organization founded by members of our team that uses music to connect student musicians with older adults and individuals experiencing cognitive decline. We wanted to explore how an organization like NOTEWORTHE could better document its activities, measure its impact, and communicate its work to potential funders.

Trace provides a centralized platform where nonprofits can record their programs, activities, sessions, participants, and measurable outcomes. Trace connects organizational data and supporting evidence directly to the narratives it generates. This allows nonprofits to turn their everyday work into structured, measurable impact that can be communicated to funders.

We built Trace using Claude Code, Tinker, Vercel, and Supabase, integrating AI to help analyze organizational information, identify measurable outcomes, and generate grant application content grounded in available evidence. We also designed the platform to make data entry and impact tracking accessible to organizations without extensive technical resources.

Throughout development, we learned that effective AI grant writing requires more than simply generating persuasive responses, since the underlying data must be organized, measurable, and traceable to credible evidence. One of our biggest challenges was designing a system that could connect individual activities and records to broader organizational outcomes without overstating or hallucinating with regards to what the data actually demonstrates., which is a super easy way to lose credibility as a nonprofit organization. We also faced challenges developing a functional prototype and integrating the different components of the platform within the time constraints of a hackathon.

Ultimately, Trace aims to give nonprofits more time to focus on their missions and less time struggling to communicate their impact. By connecting everyday activities to measurable outcomes and grant-ready evidence, we hope to make funding opportunities more accessible to the organizations that need them most.
